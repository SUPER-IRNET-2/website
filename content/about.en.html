Opening a new era of wide-field near-infrared astronomy across five continents.

SUPER-IRNET-2.0 is a JSPS Core-to-Core Program (A. Advanced Research Networks) project that runs from April 2026 through March 2031. Led by the Institute of Space and Astronautical Science (ISAS), JAXA, it builds on the ground-based network established by SUPER-IRNET-1.0 at the National Astronomical Observatory of Japan (NAOJ). Its goal is a lasting international research network that combines ground- and space-based observations and gives early-career researchers opportunities to lead.

Why wide-field near-infrared surveys?

How did the first objects in the universe form, and how did galaxies and black holes grow? With its exceptional field of view, Subaru Telescope has found numerous objects in the universe 13 billion years ago at visible wavelengths. JWST now reveals the universe only a few hundred million years after the Big Bang in remarkable detail, but its field of view is limited.

To uncover the full picture of the early universe, we must observe redshifted light in the near-infrared over wider areas, to greater depth, and at higher spatial resolution. SUPER-IRNET-2.0 combines wide surveys from space with adaptive optics and versatile instruments on the ground to overcome the barriers of wavelength coverage and Earth's atmosphere.

Three pillars

01

Coordinated observations from space and the ground

We connect ESA's Euclid and NASA's Roman with Subaru and other ground-based telescopes, combining their complementary survey area, depth, resolution, and wavelength coverage.

02

Realizing ULTIMATE-Subaru

Through multilateral technical collaboration, we develop and operate ULTIMATE, a next-generation adaptive optics system that will greatly expand Subaru's wide-field near-infrared capability.

03

Pioneering science with university-led facilities

We advance wide-field near-infrared surveys and time-domain astronomy with SWIMS on the TAO 6.5 m telescope in Chile and PRIME in South Africa.

A network across five continents

The original network linking Japan, the USA, France, Australia, and Taiwan now expands to include the UK, Korea, Chile, and South Africa. By connecting experts in space missions, ground-based telescopes, instrumentation, and data analysis, the program maximizes scientific discovery through multilateral and multi-wavelength collaboration.

Six research themes

R1 Cosmology and big data science

Combine optical data from Subaru's HSC and PFS and the Rubin Observatory with Euclid and Roman observations. Use galaxy shapes and distances to measure gravitational lensing and study the large-scale structure and expansion history of the universe.

R2 Formation of galaxies and black holes

Combine wide optical and near-infrared surveys to find galaxies and supermassive black holes from the first billion years of cosmic history. Use PRIME and SWIMS to study massive galaxies at later epochs.

R3 Infrared time-domain astronomy

Use repeated observations with Euclid, Roman and ULTIMATE to find distant supernovae and trace massive star formation in the early universe. Search nearby galaxies for transients hidden by dust.

R4 Systematic studies of the Galactic center

Use PRIME to survey microlensing events toward the Galactic center. Coordinate with Roman and ULTIMATE to measure the masses of free-floating planets and stellar-mass black holes.

R5 Development and operation of ULTIMATE

Develop wide-field adaptive optics through collaboration between Japan and Australia, and a near-infrared camera through collaboration among Japan, Taiwan and South Korea. Work across the network to plan large surveys and instrument operations.

R6 Multiwavelength collaboration and future missions

Support early-career researchers in coordinating observations across wavelengths and developing science goals and observing strategies for future missions such as JASMINE, GREX-PLUS and PRIMA.

A lasting hub connecting ground and space

SUPER-IRNET established a ground-based research hub at the National Astronomical Observatory of Japan, centered on Subaru Telescope. SUPER-IRNET-2.0 adds a new space-mission research hub centered at the JAXA Institute of Space and Astronautical Science and strengthens the connection between the two.

Discoveries from Euclid, Roman, ULTIMATE, SWIMS, and PRIME will feed into missions of the 2030s, including JASMINE, GREX-PLUS, and PRIMA. By bringing ground- and space-based communities together, the program will nurture early-career researchers capable of leading major international projects and establish a world-class network that continues beyond the funding period.

Research exchange and training

The program plans short research visits and longer stays of one to three months at partner institutions. Short visits support joint research, instrument development and presentations at international meetings. Longer stays give early-career researchers time to work with host teams on shared projects.

The program also plans international schools and workshops led by early-career researchers. By shaping research agendas and organizing meetings, participants gain experience leading international collaborations.
